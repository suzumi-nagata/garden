# The loving/lacking relationship with org-mode

I've been an Emacs user for almost 8 years now (wow, how time flies, looked like yesterday
when I didn't even know what the word IDE meant) and, for the first time in years, I'm
considering to try obsidian for a bit instead.

Personally, [org-mode](https://orgmode.org/) is my absolute favorite markup syntax
ever. It is intuitive, ergonomic, can be exported to thousands of formats and much
more. For years, I've tried to maintain "My life in Org mode", using for the agenda,
notes, ideas, journaling, etc. However, there is big hindrance when we talk about Org
mode: the portability.

When using Emacs on a conventional desktop/laptop, I feel at home. Writing feels soo easy,
easy to navigate, write and edit, all with my favorite keybindings <3. But when I'm out
somewhere, like in a mall or during a trip, There isn't much I can do to actually write in
org mode. It is already hard to find good note taking apps in general for mobile
(especially considering using a virtual keyboard), even worst is trying to find a good
note taking app that also supports org-mode.

All ports of org that I've ever seem to lack something to the point of it being more of an
annoyance than an actual good time. One of the best implementations I've saw for
**reading** org files was actually https://orgro.org/ , but it still lacks in regards to
writing features.

# Why obsidian then?

The first time I read about [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), I was already a pretty heavy
org-mode user, but it *did* caught my attention due to the polishness I saw on both
simplicity while still being super feature rich. Since I was already organizing my notes
all around org, I just left as a second thought for a tool.

A few years/months later, I saw the necessity of having a simple text editor on my phone
to make quick notes to check out on later, so I decided to install obsidian and use it as a
"scratchpad". And for a few more years/months, that was it, I had a scratchpad on my phone
for quick things that I later would transcribe to my actual notes in org.

Now what happened later was the interesting part. I recently put up my personal blog up to
publish [a post idea](https://suzumi-nagata.github.io/posts/20260221153517-ecdsa_visually_explained/) I
had during work. Since then I've been wanting a more "mobile" setup so I can take my
note taking/writing elsewhere with me. I've been especially looking at how I could write
on my phone for quick things and on my tablet for longer essays.

What ended up happening is that I bought a keyboard for my tablet and went to look for
solutions on running Emacs on android, as I saw that Emacs 30 has an official port to
it. I've looked at some tutorials, and all of them said that the best path would be to use
the tmux version of Emacs and not the native port one, as it would give me extra tools
like git to play with it.

After a lot of going back and forth, testing old packages, packages signed by other
people, downloading binaries from different places and a lot of headaches, none really
worked in the end (at least not in as satisfactory way).

One of the posts that was actually pretty hyped to make it work ~~before failing when I
actually tried~~ was the [Bring your Emacs to Android](https://snow-dev.com/posts/Emacs/bring_your_Emacs_to_android),
which got me thinking I could have almost my desktop setup on my tablet. But here is the ironic
part, despite failing to setup Emacs, I looked into the blog and found it to be a pretty nice blog layout, so I went to read
more about [Quartz](https://quartz.jzhao.xyz/).

Reading about it and seeing some examples of digital gardens made with Quartz + Obsidian
made me think about considering this change. I mean, I know Obsidian is a pretty polished
text editor, especially for mobile where it is hard to find good ones, I just didn't ever
use it because I've always used org-mode instead. So ironically, while searching about how
to use Emacs on android, the result was actually what made me push towards trying an
alternative instead.

So I created this garden and will be trying to play with Obsidian on my mobile setup and
see how it feels, who knows 🤷.

**OBS:** This isn't to say that it is a farewell to Emacs, it is still my main coding tool
that I use daily after all. When I'm on my computer, I'll still probably write markdowns
on Emacs (I may give a chance here and there for Obsidian, but using Emacs bindings is too
comfortable to change), but on mobile, I'll 100% be using obsidian to write on the go.

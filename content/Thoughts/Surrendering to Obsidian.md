I've been an emacs user for almost 8 years now (wow, time flies, looks like yesterday when
I was writing my first hello world in C) and, for the first time in years, I'm considering
to try obsidian for a bit instead.

![[gardener.png]]

Personally, org-mode is my absolute favorite markup syntax ever. It is intuitive,
ergonomic, can be exported to thousands of formats and much more. For years, I've tried to
maintain "My life in [Org mode](https://orgmode.org/)", using the agenda, for notes,
ideas, journaling, etc. However, there is big hindrance when we talk about Org mode: the
portability.

When using emacs on a conventional desktop, I fell at home. Writing feels soo easy. Easy
to navigate, write, edit, all with my favorite keybindings. But when I'm out somewhere, on
a mall or on a trip, There isn't much I can do to actually write in emacs or even org
mode. All ports of org that I've ever seen lack something to the point of it being more of
an annoyance than and actual good time.

One of the best implementations I've saw for **reading** org files was actually
https://orgro.org/ , but it still lacks in regards to writing features.

The irony of this change was that I was actually looking into alternatives to use emacs on
the go. I've saw that emacs 30 has been ported to android and heard good things about it
being able to integrate with tmux and other tools.

I've tried following this guide:
https://snow-dev.com/posts/Emacs/bring_your_emacs_to_android, but after a week of
attempts, I think it is too much hassle for what I wanted. As a coincidence, while reading
about this post about emacs on android, I really liked the quartz library and decided to
try.

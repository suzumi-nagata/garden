- Tell them what you are going to say (introduction).
- Tell them what you have to say (content).
- Tell them what you have just said (conclusions).
- Although not mandatory, the use of presentation tools (such as LibreOffice slides, PowerPoint, LaTeX, etc.) is recommended. When using slides, remember to:
  - try to keep between 0.8 and 1 slide per minute of presentation;
  - prepare a few extra slides that clarify points not covered in detail during the presentation due to time constraints to be used during the Q&A session (anticipate possible questions from the audience);
  - use images instead of words whenever possible;
  - be brief and precise in your messages (long phrases or sentences are counterproductive and will compromise your message ==> use short, but intelligible phrases as if they were newspaper headlines);
  - use the largest possible font and image sizes (do not leave unused white space: it is a waste and compromises the quality of the presentation);
  - include a maximum of 10 lines of text per slide (if there are no images);
  - each slide should have its own title, which should be coherent and representative of the content;
  - use all available space on the slide, avoiding the concentration of text and/or images in a single area (if there is extra space, enlarge the image or font, but do not add more text).
- Rehearse your presentation until it fits within the allotted time. Keep in mind that, on
  the day of the presentation, anxiety, pressure and other factors can influence on the
  real presentation, the more you rehearse, the more consistent you can be, even with
  adversities
